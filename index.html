THE RAIL FENCE CIPHER

TRANSPOSITION CRYPTOGRAPHY PROTOCOL

01 // The Core Concept

The Rail Fence Cipher (also known as the Zigzag Cipher) is a classical transposition cipher. Unlike substitution ciphers (such as Caesar), the characters themselves are never changed or replaced. Instead, their positions are rearranged along a geometrical zigzag path across a set number of parallel "rails".

02 // The Zigzag Geometry

With a Key of 3 Rails, characters bounce between the top rail (APEX) and bottom rail (NADIR). For the message HELLOWORLD: 

RAIL 0:  H . . . O . . . L .   -->  H O L
RAIL 1:  . E . L . W . R . D   -->  E L W R D
RAIL 2:  . . L . . . O . . .   -->  L O

COLLECTED CIPHERTEXT: H O L + E L W R D + L O  =  HOLELWRDLO

03 // Deterministic Reversal (Decryption)

To reconstruct the original message, the system calculates the length of the ciphertext and traces the empty zigzag path. It calculates exactly how many characters belong to each rail, places the ciphertext letters into their respective rail rows, and then the Keykeeper traces the zigzag path to read out the letters in original chronological order.
